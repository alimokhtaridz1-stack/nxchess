# NX Chess website plan

## Goal

Present NX Chess as a professional chess teaching center. Visitors should understand the programs, learning path, people, teaching approach, and how to get in touch.

The site should feel complete, established in its presentation, and welcoming. Professional appearance must come from useful information and consistent design, never invented credentials or exaggerated claims.

## Scope

Seven core public pages: Home, Programs, Curriculum, Coaches, About, FAQs, and Contact.
Gallery and Events are conditional additions when real approved content exists. All pages are accessible without signing in.
The site introduces the center and invites inquiries; no accounts, payments, online lessons, or booking system.

Working audience assumption: learners and parents considering chess instruction. Confirm ages served, levels, location, languages, and online or in-person availability before final copy. Calling NX Chess a teaching center does not establish that it has physical premises; do not invent an address or facilities.

## Navigation and routes

| Navbar label | Route | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | / | Introduce the center and connect its main offerings |
| Programs | /programs | Explain available teaching formats and who they serve |
| Curriculum | /curriculum | Show learning stages, topics, practice, and outcomes |
| Coaches | /coaches | Introduce real teachers and their verified experience |
| About | /about | Tell the center story and explain its teaching approach |
| FAQs | /faq | Answer common practical questions |
| Contact us | /contact | Prominent button to reach the team |

The NX Chess logo also links to Home. Contact us is the single emphasized action in the header.

When Gallery or Events has approved content, About becomes a link plus a separate dropdown toggle. The menu contains "About NX Chess" (/about), "Gallery" (/gallery), and/or "Events" (/events). Show only published destinations. Without these additions, About remains a normal link.

Do not put a long list of speculative programs in the navbar. Program names can link to sections within /programs once actual offerings are confirmed.
On narrower screens, collapse the whole navigation into an accessible mobile menu before it wraps or becomes cramped. Every destination, including any dropdown children, must remain available.

## Visitor journeys

- New visitor: Home → Programs → Curriculum → Contact.
- Parent: Home → Coaches → FAQs → Contact.
- Learner comparing options: Programs → relevant curriculum stage → Contact.
- Visitor learning about the center: About → Gallery or Events, if available → Contact.
- Returning visitor: any page → Contact us → preferred verified channel.

## Page structure

### Home — a complete introduction to the center

1. Full shared navigation.
2. Hero with a clear introduction and authentic imagery. Primary link: "Explore our programs"; secondary: "Talk to us."
3. A concise introduction to NX Chess and its teaching approach.
4. Programs preview linking to actual offerings.
5. Curriculum progression preview.
6. Meet the coaches preview using verified profiles.
7. Life at the center: approved photographs or an actual event preview, when available.
8. A few useful FAQ answers linking to the full page.
9. Clear contact invitation and complete footer.

Use varied layouts and purposeful section spacing. Avoid filling missing sections with fake testimonials or statistics.

### Programs — understand the teaching options

1. Short explanation of the available offerings.
2. Overview of each confirmed program: audience, starting level, format, learning focus, and how to inquire.
3. A practical comparison when multiple programs exist; compare only known details.
4. Links to relevant curriculum sections and coach profiles.
5. "Ask about this program" links to Contact with readable context.

Potential categories for owner review include children, adults, group classes, private coaching, and tournament preparation. These are suggestions, not confirmed offerings. Publish only actual services. If one program is offered, present it thoroughly without inventing others.
No enrollment, checkout, live availability, or automated placement.

### Curriculum — understand the learning path

1. How the learning path works.
2. Linked stage navigation.
3. Stage details: who it is for, topics, practice examples, and observable outcomes.
4. How stages relate to confirmed programs.
5. Explain that starting level is discussed with the team.
6. "Not sure where to start? Talk to us."

Use the proposed structure in CONTENT.md until the actual curriculum is confirmed.

### Coaches — meet the teachers

1. Brief team introduction.
2. Each real coach's approved portrait, name, role, bio, teaching focus, and verified experience.
3. Connection to relevant programs where known.
4. Contact invitation.

If the center has one coach, present one complete profile. Do not invent staff to imply a larger team.

### About — understand the center

1. The actual NX Chess story and purpose.
2. Teaching philosophy with concrete examples.
3. What learners can expect in a lesson, once confirmed.
4. Real teaching environment or delivery format; location only if supplied.
5. Links to Coaches, Programs, and conditional Gallery or Events.
6. Contact invitation.

### FAQs — remove practical uncertainty

Group verified questions by getting started, programs and curriculum, lesson arrangements, and contacting the team.
Answers should be concise, accessible, and link to relevant pages. Do not invent age eligibility, trial lessons, equipment policies, schedules, fees, or response times.

### Contact — reach the center

1. Friendly invitation to ask about programs or the curriculum.
2. Verified WhatsApp, email, telephone, or social channels.
3. Location, directions link, hours, and lesson format only when confirmed.
4. A brief suggestion of what to include in an inquiry, without sensitive student information.

No form by default. Clearly identify links that open WhatsApp, email, calls, or external directions.

### Conditional Gallery — see real center activity

Use approved photographs with useful captions and appropriate permission. Show real teaching or activities. Avoid empty albums or stock imagery presented as the center.

### Conditional Events — discover actual activities

Describe confirmed workshops, tournaments, or open days only if offered. Include accurate dates, time zone, place or online format, audience, status, and inquiry link. Separate upcoming from past activities and remove stale invitations. No invented calendar, ticketing, or registration system.

## Shared website structure

All pages use the same header, active navigation states, footer, colors, typography, button styles, and contact details.
The footer groups Explore (Home, About, Coaches), Learn (Programs, Curriculum, FAQs), and Contact; include Gallery and Events only when published.
Inner pages have descriptive titles, short introductions, a deliberate content layout, relevant cross-links, and a contact invitation. Optional breadcrumbs support deeper navigation.

## Phases after implementation is requested

1. Content foundation: confirm offerings, curriculum, coaches, center story, FAQ answers, and contact details.
2. Design foundation: establish the shared shell and preview Home plus a representative inner page.
3. Public pages: complete all seven core pages with working cross-links.
4. Conditional content: add Gallery and Events only when supported by approved material.
5. Quality review: inspect every page on mobile and desktop; verify accessibility, content, navigation, and contact actions.
6. Launch: publish to the approved domain and hosting destination when authorized.

## Definition of done

All seven core pages are useful and fully styled. The navigation clearly exposes the center's key information without crowding. The curriculum is public and contact channels work. No incomplete inner pages, dead-end buttons, fabricated content, or draft placeholders are published.
