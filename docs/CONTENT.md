## Proposed curriculum structure

Do not claim a particular duration, age band, rating range, certificate, or guaranteed result. Stages describe learning progression; the owner must confirm the real curriculum.

### 1. First moves

For: someone new to chess.
Topics: board setup, piece movement, captures, check, checkmate, stalemate, and basic legal moves.
Practice: identify legal moves and play simple guided positions.
Observable outcomes: set up a board, move pieces legally, and distinguish checkmate from stalemate.

### 2. Build good habits

For: a learner who knows the rules and is starting full games.
Topics: king safety, development, center control, undefended pieces, and basic opening principles.
Practice: compare candidate moves and check for threats before moving.
Observable outcomes: explain a simple opening plan and notice common immediate threats.

### 3. Spot tactics

For: a learner ready to recognize recurring tactical ideas.
Topics: forks, pins, skewers, discovered attacks, removing defenders, and elementary mating patterns.
Practice: solve positions and explain why a sequence works.
Observable outcomes: identify a tactical motif and check the opponent's possible replies.

### 4. Make a plan

For: a learner developing a more deliberate middlegame approach.
Topics: piece activity, pawn structure, weak squares, exchanges, and choosing candidate moves.
Practice: compare positions and propose a short plan with a reason.
Observable outcomes: describe a position's main features and connect a move to a plan.

### 5. Finish and review

For: a learner combining earlier ideas in complete games.
Topics: basic pawn endings, opposition, king activity, elementary checkmates, time management, and game review.
Practice: play simplified endings and annotate a short game.
Observable outcomes: demonstrate selected basic endings and identify a useful lesson from a played game.

These stages can be revised, combined, or removed to match the actual teaching program.
